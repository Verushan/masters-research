ZSC-Eval · MORL benchmark · run report

Zero-shot coordination across three kitchens

A multi-objective reward matches hand-crafted shaping when an agent plays itself, and loses that advantage when it meets a stranger. Measured on three Overcooked layouts against a common held-out partner set — one of which turned out not to be a valid partner set at all.

Layouts random0 · random3 · unident_s Arms 4 × 3 seeds × 2e6 steps Partners 16 HSP bias agents per layout Cross-play 48415 · 48439 · 48468 Ladder 3 rungs × 3 seeds × 2 layouts Date 31 Aug 2026

What was measured

Four stage-1 arms differ only in the reward the PPO buffer sees: the ZSC-Eval baseline (sparse + shaped), sparse alone, and the objective vector w · r_vec with fixed and with mirror-descent adaptive weights. Every arm is then played against every other and against 16 held-out HSP bias agents, in both seats. The zero-shot number is the mean over that held-out set.

The headline

replicated on both usable layouts

Self-play skill does not survive the introduction

On random0 the MORL arms beat the hand-shaped baseline in self-play by a wide margin — 227–240 against 133 — and then score a third of its zero-shot return. On unident_s they draw level in self-play (453 vs 460) and still lose zero-shot. The sign is the same on both layouts and in both checkpoint variants: the objective vector buys self-play competence that does not transfer.

random3 is not measuring anything

partner set invalid

The held-out agents cannot cook alone

Counter Circuit returned near-zero for every arm, which looked like undertraining — the arms are undertrained, with only the hand-shaped baseline getting all three seeds to deliver at 2e6 steps. But the deeper problem is the partner set. An HSP bias agent is a pair: w0 carries the biased reward, w1 is a plain sparse partner trained beside it, and the logged return is the team's. Convention evaluates against w0 alone.

On random3 the w0 agents put 0.2 onions in the pot and cook in 2 of 18 episodes — they never start the task — while training as a pair returned a healthy 164. w1 carried the team and w0 specialised on its bias. Every cell involving a partner is therefore structurally zero no matter how good the ego agent is.

The obvious explanation — role asymmetry starves w0 — is wrong: random0 has the sharpest asymmetry of the three and its w0 agents work (5.2 onions, cooking in 9/16). The likelier account is that Counter Circuit is the one layout a single agent can complete unilaterally, so w1 was free to carry the pair. Forced coordination protects the bias agent from freeloading precisely because the task cannot be done alone. That is testable and untested.

Consequence: retraining the random3 arms at 1e7 steps — about 105 node-hours — would fix the arms and leave the metric exactly as uninformative. The layout needs its bias agents rebuilt, or evaluated as w0+w1 pairs, first.

Does knowing your partner help?

replicated on both layouts

No — and the cheap encoding actively hurts

A stage-2 agent normally sees its partner's identity only through the centralised critic. The ladder feeds it to the actor as well — as the raw scalar the critic gets, and as a one-hot over the population — and measures against the training population, where the identity is truthful. This is an oracle upper bound, not a ZSC method: a held-out partner has no id the agent was ever trained on. Its value is separating partner uncertainty from partner diversity.

The raw scalar costs return on both layouts, in all six seed pairs, in the same direction — t = 3.93 and 3.36. The one-hot is indistinguishable from the control on both (t = 0.94, 0.26). Telling the actor who its partner is buys nothing even when the identity is truthful and the partner was trained against; the only measurable effect of the ladder is the damage done by the cheaper encoding.

That damage is the encoding fault the flag's own documentation predicts. load_population assigns id = (i + 1) / num_policies, so partners adjacent in the yml get adjacent ids and the network is handed a continuous feature over what is a categorical variable. One-hotting removes the false ordering — and once it is removed there is simply nothing left to gain.

The floor-effect caveat is now answered. The random0 agents reach ~73 on a layout where stage-1 self-play cross-plays to zero, so the first null could have been an agent too weak to exploit what it was told. The unident_s agents reach ~192 and give the same answer. The stage-2 coordination deficit is not partner uncertainty.

What had to be fixed to get here

Two of these silently produce a result rather than an error, which is why they are worth recording.

  • No layout but random0 could be cross-played at all. Every yml entry names a policy_config/*.pkl that the evaluator rebuilds each frozen policy from, and its spaces are layout-shaped — (5,5,20), (8,5,20), (9,5,20) — so it cannot be copied across. Upstream produces it by training two throwaway agents for 1e7 steps each. prep/store_policy_config.py builds the same tuple from the env constructor in seconds, verified identical to random0's on every network-shaping argument.
  • The evaluator defaulted to a partner pool that exists only for random0. On any other layout every entry was dropped by --skip_missing, the matrix came out with no held-out column, and the analysis asserted on an empty partner group — after both cross-play passes had been paid for. HELDOUT=hsp now selects the bias agents and the matching partner group.
  • The ladder existed only as a shell one-liner, which is how its first seeds came to be trained at 12 rollout threads while .env says 4. That variable is the PPO batch size, so the comparison would have been a batch-size result. pipelines/pid-ladder.sh pins it.
  • Peak-checkpoint extraction earned its keep. On random3 the only MORL seed that ever learned to deliver peaked at 166.7 and decayed to 56.7; the final checkpoint would have discarded it.

Where this leaves the programme

  • unident_s is the only layout of the three that yields a graded ZSC signal. random0 floors every method at a worst case of 0.0; random3's partner set does not function. Cross-layout claims currently rest on two layouts, one of them near the floor.
  • The negative result on MORL is the solid one. Same sign, both layouts, both checkpoint variants: the objective vector does not improve zero-shot coordination over hand-crafted shaping. It matches it in self-play, which is worth reporting on its own.
  • Adaptive versus fixed weights is unresolved. Adaptive leads on unident_s by 33 points with a ±46.9 spread, and the two are indistinguishable on random0. Deciding it needs more seeds, not another layout.
  • Partner conditioning is closed. The oracle ceiling is not above the control on either layout, so the remaining gap is partner diversity, not partner uncertainty. Whatever repairs stage-2 coordination, it is not more information about who the partner is.
  • Rebuild random3's bias agents before spending on its arms. More steps alone fixes the wrong half.

Cross-play: eval/cross_play.py, deterministic (1 episode per ordered pair, the full distribution under argmax) and stochastic (5 episodes, the only pass that can report return stability).
